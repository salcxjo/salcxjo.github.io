Running

I played basketball for years. When I moved to Canada it all seemed to difficult to get back into. Life came at me fast. Running filled the gap, not immediately, but gradually.

In Red Deer, the trail systems cut through river valleys and ravines. Going out there became a kind of sanctuary. Gaetz Lake was were I first run on single trails. The problems you carry tend to resolve themselves somewhere around the third kilometre.

It's never been about performance. It's grounding and meditative. It's a way of thinking that happens to involve moving your legs. I like the nerdy side too: biomechanics, pacing theory, why easy runs make you faster. I fall into those rabbit holes happily.

I run alone mostly, but community matters. Run clubs, park runs, the quiet nod between two people out early on Sask Drive. a small culture and a good one.

Walking

There is a version of travel that moves too fast to see anything. Walking is the correction. The pace at which the world was meant to be looked at.

Humans evolved as persistence hunters — not fast, but relentless. Built to walk long distances and notice things. The noticing is the point. You don't understand a place from a car. You understand it on foot, slowly, over many visits.

I have specific walks for specific days — a long one on Sundays when there's nothing that needs to happen quickly, a shorter loop mid-week. They're not routes so much as rhythms: familiar enough that the legs move without instruction, which frees the mind to do its other work.

some walks are meant for thinking, others for noticing — the skill is knowing which one you need

thinking
The thinking walk No destination. The problem you're carrying usually resolves somewhere on the walk.
noticing
The noticing walk Slow. Eyes open. The one where you stop to look at things, a roof line, a tree you've passed a hundred times.
decompress
The decompression walk After something difficult. The body moving is enough. You don't need to arrive anywhere.
explore
The exploration walk A new neighbourhood, a new city. No shortcuts. Get a little lost on purpose.
early
The early walk Before most people are moving. The world is still soft. You get it briefly to yourself.

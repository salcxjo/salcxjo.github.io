Kayaking

Fourteen days on water, twice. The kind of time that makes the rest of life feel a little less urgent.

Trip I
14 days  ·  ~2022
Dublin Bay &
Ironbound Island
Nova Scotia  ·  Atlantic Coast

Launching from Dublin Bay, paddling the rugged Atlantic coast around Ironbound Island and a scatter of smaller ones. Fog, swells, spruce shoreline, the particular silence of open ocean camping.

Day 1  ·  Dublin Bay launch
The first day is always strange. You're still thinking about what you forgot to pack. By evening you've stopped. The Atlantic doesn't care about your checklist — it just is, and you paddle into it.
Ironbound Island
Bigger than it looks on the chart. We rounded it slowly, hugging the lee side, watching the kelp beds go by. Nobody else out there.
On tides
Atlantic tides move fast and don't negotiate. You learn to read the water or you spend the day fighting it. Eventually it becomes instinct.

Trip II
14 days  ·  ~2024
Clayoquot
Sound
Vancouver Island  ·  BC  ·  Pacific

Launching from Tofino into one of the most intact temperate rainforests left on the planet. Old growth, open Pacific swells, orca country. The scale of things out here recalibrates you.

Bearing northwest. Map bungeed to the deck, water bottle wedged in. The mountains don't get closer for a long time.
Clayoquot Sound  ·  mid-channel
Threading the intertidal zone at low water. The rocks are green all the way down. You can see the bottom through the hull.
intertidal  ·  ~0.3m clearance
On paddling slowly
The urge on day one is to cover distance. By day four you've slowed to match the water. The rocks worth looking at are the ones you nearly missed.
Camp. The fire was small and didn't need to be bigger. We sat until the light was completely gone.
beach camp  ·  low tide evening
After sunset, before dark. That particular blue that only lasts ten minutes. You stop whatever you're doing.
dusk  ·  somewhere quiet
A side trip into old growth. The cedar is several hundred years old. People become small in a useful way.
Meares Island  ·  old growth trail
14 days later
The thing about a long paddle trip is the re-entry. You come back and the city is the same but you're slightly different — slower, more patient with silence. It takes about a week to wear off. You start planning the next one.
